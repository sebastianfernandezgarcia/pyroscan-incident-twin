# Demo video script — 2:40 target

Format: 16:9, 1080p, public YouTube video, spoken English, captions enabled.

## 0:00–0:18 — The premise

**Screen:** Full PyroScan board. Hold on the real La Palma silhouette, live fire/smoke and moving attention contours, then point to the evidence and decision panes.

**Voiceover:**

> Emergency exercises do not need an AI that clicks faster. They need a person and an agent to reason over the same evidence without losing human control. This is PyroScan Incident Twin, a shared wildfire rehearsal table powered by WebMCP.

## 0:18–0:34 — Domain honesty

**Screen:** Highlight the synthetic-exercise ribbon and local-first label.

**Voiceover:**

> Everything here is deterministic and synthetic: no live incident, no dispatch, and no hidden backend. The contours are exercise attention areas, never forecasts. The human remains the exercise director.

## 0:34–0:49 — Show native Site Tools

**Screen:** Open Site Tools in the browser bar. Show the six tool names for two seconds.

**Voiceover:**

> The page exposes six narrow tools directly to ChatGPT: read the board, inspect a zone, simulate, compare, annotate and stage a reversible plan. They use the same state as the visible interface.

## 0:49–1:02 — Prompt

**Screen:** Send this prompt:

> Inspect El Paso. Add a blocked-road exercise note for the LP-3 checkpoint. Simulate a 60-minute northeast wind shift, compare the ridge and dual-interface options, then stage the safest reversible plan.

**Voiceover:**

> I can ask at the level of intent instead of describing a chain of clicks.

## 1:02–1:28 — Human knowledge joins the board

**Screen:** `inspect_zone`, then `add_board_annotation`. Show the selected map marker and the amber blocked-road card.

**Voiceover:**

> ChatGPT focuses El Paso. Then it adds a bounded exercise note. That local knowledge becomes visible to me and available to every later tool call.

## 1:28–1:52 — Scenario and comparison

**Screen:** `simulate_spread`, then `compare_response_options`. Let the contour animate and show the reranked cards.

**Voiceover:**

> A deterministic northeast-shift scenario moves the contours on our shared map. The response comparison now accounts for the route constraint, setup time, coverage and reserve use. The result is inspectable, not buried in chat.

## 1:52–2:15 — Safe staging and lineage

**Screen:** `read_incident_board`, then `stage_response_plan`. Zoom on board version and the plan's access action.

**Voiceover:**

> Before staging, ChatGPT reads the current board version. A stale version is rejected. The plan explicitly says to resolve the recorded El Paso route constraint, proving that human knowledge changed the downstream artifact.

## 2:15–2:32 — Human-only gate

**Screen:** Hover over “Approve exercise plan” but do not click immediately. Show the caption that approval is not a WebMCP tool, then click as the human.

**Voiceover:**

> ChatGPT can stage, but it cannot approve. Only I can complete the decision gate in the human interface. Every proposal stays reversible until then.

## 2:32–2:40 — Close

**Screen:** Final approved plan and map, then project title.

**Voiceover:**

> PyroScan makes the browser a common operating surface for human judgment and agent exploration. WebMCP is not an add-on here; it is the collaboration protocol.

## Recording checklist

- Keep the Site Tools panel visible long enough for judges to read names.
- Capture the contour motion and the activity timeline updates.
- Let the fire, smoke and ember animation read for at least two seconds in the opening shot.
- Ensure the browser header reads **6 tools live**.
- Do not exceed 2:55 after editing.
- Export with audible speech and captions.
